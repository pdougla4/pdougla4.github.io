Senior Design — Subsurface Modeling and Uncertainty Analysis analysis complete

This senior-design project evaluated underground hydrogen storage in the Red River Formation of the Williston Basin using a laboratory-constrained computational workflow for subsurface operating conditions in a fractured carbonate reservoir. The project combined analytical pressure and thermal modeling, geomechanical and capillary constraints, reservoir simulation, and uncertainty quantification.

Show technical details
  • Scientific computing: Built a fast closed-form dual-porosity pressure and thermal model in Python using analytical solutions and numerical inversion.
  • Surrogate modeling: Developed and validated a computationally efficient surrogate for rapid evaluation across large uncertainty and operating-condition ensembles.
  • Engineering analysis: Integrated pressure, temperature, capillary behavior, and rock mechanical constraints into a unified operating-envelope workflow.
  • Uncertainty quantification: Used Latin hypercube sampling, physical pre-filtering, Morris screening, and Sobol sensitivity analysis to quantify uncertainty and rank influential parameters.
  • Decision support: Generated P50 and P90 boundaries and conservative classification rules for identifying acceptable, marginal, and unacceptable operating conditions.
  • Laboratory integration: Incorporated permeability, NMR, wettability, contact-angle, and interfacial-tension measurements from reservoir and caprock samples.
  • Reservoir simulation: Built and evaluated a CMG/GEM compositional sector model to assess fluid injection, production response, recovery, and subsurface storage behavior.
  • Value of information: Identified the parameters contributing most strongly to model uncertainty and prioritized measurements that would most improve future predictions.
  • Reproducibility: Structured the analysis as a documented, repeatable workflow connecting raw inputs, model assumptions, uncertainty propagation, and final engineering outputs.
